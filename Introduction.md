Writing unit tests manually is time-consuming and error-prone, requiring developers to construct cases that exercise different execution paths, boundary conditions, and exception handling. Under tight schedules and limited resources, this work is often skipped or done superficially, which leaves many software projects with thin test suites and, over time, makes maintenance harder and reduces overall software quality.

Large language models have been applied to this problem with mixed but often encouraging results. Wang et al. (2024) combined GPT-3.5-turbo with method slicing and self-debugging in their HITS framework, reaching an average branch coverage of 48.12% on complex Java methods (CC > 10) — well above EvoSuite's 38.46% on the same benchmark. Tang et al. (2024), by contrast, found that a zero-shot ChatGPT (GPT-3, early 2023) still trailed EvoSuite noticeably in statement coverage (55.4% vs. 74.2%) and in exception-bug detection, suggesting that raw LLM output without additional scaffolding does not automatically beat search-based tools. Ryan et al. (2024) showed that a code-aware prompting strategy (SymPrompt), which extracts path constraints via static AST traversal, could push branch coverage on 897 Python focal methods from 44% up to 66% after filtering — still short of Pynguin's 64% baseline on the same set. On the mutation-testing side, Guerino and Vincenzi (2025) reported that GPT-3.5-turbo with an error-feedback loop reached a mutation score of 91.5% on small Python programs, clearly outperforming Pynguin (69.8%), while Aminata et al. (2025) found their Mistral-7B-based two-step prompting approach won on mutation score (41.3% vs. 30.8%) despite lower raw coverage than EvoSuite. More recently, Cabral et al. (2025) compared GPT-4o against DeepSeek V3 on six Java classes of increasing cyclomatic complexity (17–77) and found GPT-4o ahead on compilation success rate (63.33% vs. 50%), though DeepSeek produced higher mutation scores when its tests did compile. At a larger scale, Huang et al. (2026) built the ULT benchmark specifically to control for data leakage, and showed that reported LLM performance on older, contaminated benchmarks can be substantially inflated — Pass@5 dropped from 44.45% on a leaked benchmark to just 12.57% on their leak-free version.

However, none of these eight studies has evaluated a lightweight, off-the-shelf model such as GPT-4o mini in a pure zero-shot setting — that is, without additional scaffolding like method slicing, AST-based path extraction, or chain-of-thought prompting — on functions isolated specifically within the 5–15 cyclomatic complexity range, while jointly reporting both branch coverage and mutation score against a consistent baseline. Existing work instead relies on larger models (GPT-4, GPT-4o) or on more elaborate frameworks (HITS, SymPrompt, 2SZSP), uses complexity ranges that are either much higher (CC > 10 in HITS, CC ≥ 10 in ULT) or unfiltered altogether, and in several cases does not report a mutation score at all. As a result, it remains unclear how a lightweight model performs on moderately complex Python functions when evaluated under one consistent, reproducible framework.

In this paper, we present an empirical evaluation of GPT-4o mini for zero-shot unit test generation on Python functions with different levels of cyclomatic complexity, evaluating the generated tests along four dimensions: branch coverage, mutation score, correctness, and test generation time.
•	To summarize, this paper contributes:
 The first empirical evaluation of GPT-4o mini for zero-shot unit test generation on Python functions across different cyclomatic complexity levels.

•	 Experimental results measuring branch coverage, mutation score, correctness, and test generation time under one consistent evaluation framework.

•	 A discussion of the practical strengths and limitations of GPT-4o mini for automated unit testing, including guidance on where lightweight models are and are not sufficient.
